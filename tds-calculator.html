Budget 2026: What’s New in TDS – Complete Guide for FY 2026-27

The Union Budget 2026 continued the rationalisation of Tax Deducted at Source provisions while the new Income-tax Act, 2025 became operational from 1 April 2026. For Financial Year 2026-27 the core TDS architecture remains familiar, but several thresholds, rates and compliance timelines have been clarified or modestly adjusted. This guide explains every major section that a CA, business owner, freelancer or salary earner needs for day-to-day compliance.

Section 192 – TDS on Salary

Employers must deduct tax on salary as per the applicable income-tax slab under the regime chosen by the employee (new regime is default). Standard deduction of ₹75,000 under the new regime or ₹50,000 under the old regime is factored into the computation. Form 16 is issued annually. Monthly TDS is deposited by the 7th of the following month.

Section 194A – Interest other than Interest on Securities

Banks, co-operative societies and post offices deduct 10% TDS on interest. Threshold has been enhanced in recent years (commonly ₹50,000 for senior citizens and higher limits for others in many cases). Without PAN the rate jumps to 20%. FD interest calculators of SBI, HDFC and other banks use this section.

Section 194C – Payments to Contractors

1% for individual/HUF contractors and 2% for others. Threshold: ₹30,000 per contract or ₹1,00,000 aggregate in a financial year. Widely used for civil work, transport, advertising and job-work payments.

Section 194I – Rent

2% on rent of plant & machinery and 10% on rent of land, building or furniture. Monthly threshold of ₹50,000 applies in updated rules. Individuals and HUFs not liable for tax audit are covered under 194IB at 2% when monthly rent exceeds ₹50,000.

Section 194J – Professional or Technical Fees

10% on professional fees and 2% on technical services, call-centre services, royalty and film distribution in many cases. Threshold ₹50,000 in a year. Freelancers, CAs, doctors, architects and software consultants fall under this section.

Section 194Q – Purchase of Goods

Buyer whose turnover exceeds ₹10 crore must deduct 0.1% TDS when purchases from a resident seller exceed ₹50 lakh in a year. This is a turnover-based compliance that many trading and manufacturing concerns must track carefully.

Section 194O – E-commerce

E-commerce operators deduct 0.1% on the gross amount of sale or service facilitated. Threshold ₹5 lakh for individuals/HUFs in certain cases. Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho and similar platforms operate under this section.

Section 194S – Crypto & Virtual Digital Assets

1% TDS on transfer of VDAs (cryptocurrency, NFTs etc.). Threshold ₹10,000 generally and ₹50,000 for specified persons. Without PAN the rate is 20%. Exchanges such as WazirX, CoinDCX and others deduct this at source. Section 194S overrides 194O when both apply.

Section 195 – Payments to Non-Residents / NRI

Any sum chargeable to tax in India paid to a non-resident attracts TDS under 195. Rates depend on the nature of income and applicable DTAA. Common rates for freelancers on Upwork/Fiverr-type platforms range from 10% to 30% plus surcharge and cess if treaty relief is not claimed. Form 15CA/15CB may be required.

No-PAN and Higher Rate Provisions

Section 206AA mandates higher TDS (generally 20%) when PAN is not furnished. Section 206AB further increases the rate for specified persons who have not filed returns for relevant years. Always verify PAN and filing status before finalising the rate.

Late Deposit – Interest & Fee

Interest under section 201(1A) is 1% per month or part of the month from the date tax was deductible to the date of actual deduction, and 1.5% thereafter until deposit. Fee under section 234E is ₹200 per day of default, subject to the amount of TDS. Correct and timely deposit remains the cheapest compliance strategy.

Maintain a section-wise ledger, track aggregate payments against thresholds, collect PAN and declarations early, reconcile with Form 26AS and AIS every quarter, and use bulk tools for large vendor lists. No PAN TDS 2026: Section 206AA & 206AB Explained

This is where most people lose money. tds calculator without pan 2026 must consider 2 rules:

  1. Section 206AA: If deductee does not provide PAN, TDS is deducted at higher of: Prescribed rate OR 20%.
  2. Section 206AB: If deductee is a "Specified Person" i.e. has not filed ITR for last 2 years and TDS was more than ₹50,000, then TDS is higher of: Twice the prescribed rate OR 5%.
