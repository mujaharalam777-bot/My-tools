Complete Guide to Education Loan in 2026

By Mujahar, Financial Expert

An education loan is often the bridge between a student’s ambition and the actual fee of a good college or university. In 2026 the product is more nuanced than a simple “take money, pay EMI later” story. Moratorium rules, interest capitalization, collateral thresholds, tax relief under section 80E and currency risk for study-abroad programmes all affect the real cost. This guide walks through the practical points most families overlook when they first sit with a bank or NBFC.

How EMI is built after the study period

During the course and a short grace period (together called the moratorium), many lenders allow you to pay only interest or even nothing. If you pay nothing, the unpaid interest is usually added to the principal. That larger figure then becomes the base for EMI calculation over the repayment tenure. The difference between “service interest during study” and “let it capitalize” can run into several lakhs on a multi-year foreign degree. Use the slider on this page to see the gap for your own numbers.

Collateral-free limits and when security is needed

Most mainstream schemes in India still offer a collateral-free window for smaller loans. Once the requirement crosses that band, banks ask for property papers, fixed deposits or a third-party guarantee. The presence of security sometimes improves the interest rate, but it also adds documentation time. Non-collateral loans are faster to disburse yet may carry a slightly higher spread.

Section 80E – the tax angle many forget

Interest paid on an education loan for higher studies is deductible under section 80E. There is no upper limit on the interest amount and the deduction can be claimed for a maximum of eight years (or until the interest is fully paid, whichever is earlier). Principal repayment does not get this benefit. Keeping clean interest certificates from the lender each year makes the claim straightforward at the time of filing returns.

Study abroad and exchange rates

When fees are billed in dollars, pounds or euros, a weaker rupee increases the rupee outflow even if the foreign-currency fee stays the same. Some students lock part of the cost early through education loans that disburse in stages. Others keep a buffer for currency movement. The forex slider here is only an illustration; actual hedging products, if any, should be discussed with the bank.

Choosing between public sector banks, private banks and specialised lenders

Public sector banks often start with competitive base rates and familiar processes. Private banks and education-focused NBFCs may score on speed, digital tracking and flexibility for foreign universities. Prodigy-style or specialised cross-border lenders sometimes price on future earning potential rather than parental collateral. There is no single “best” lender for every profile; the right choice depends on the course location, the family’s existing relationship with a bank, and how much documentation you are willing to handle.

A practical checklist before you sign

Confirm the exact moratorium length and whether interest is simple or compounding during that period. Ask for the full schedule of processing fees, inspection charges and prepayment rules. Check whether the loan covers living expenses or only tuition. Keep scholarship award letters ready so the bank can reduce the sanctioned amount and you do not over-borrow. Finally, run the numbers once with interest servicing during study and once without — the EMI gap is often larger than families expect.

This page is for education and planning only. Interest rates, eligibility and tax rules can change. Speak with the lender and, if needed, a qualified advisor before making a decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every education loan offer a moratorium?

Most full-time higher-education loans include a study period plus a short grace period. Exact length and whether interest is charged or deferred varies by lender and scheme.

Can I get an education loan without a guarantor?

Collateral-free schemes up to a limit often do not insist on a third-party guarantor, though the bank still evaluates the co-applicant’s income and credit history.

Is interest subsidy still available?

Central and some state schemes have offered interest subsidy for eligible students during the moratorium. Eligibility criteria and continuation of any scheme should be verified with the bank for the current year.

How much can I claim under 80E?

There is no monetary ceiling on the interest amount. The deduction is available for a maximum of eight consecutive years starting from the year you begin repaying interest.
